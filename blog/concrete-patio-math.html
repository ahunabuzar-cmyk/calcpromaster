The 4-inch mistake — why concrete orders go wrong

Sep 16, 2026 · ~6 min read · Home

Quick answer: most concrete ordering disasters are unit errors, not math errors. Type thickness as "4" meaning 4 feet when you mean 4 inches, and a 20×20 patio's order inflates 12×— from about 5.4 cubic yards to 59. Here is the whole calculation, done once, slowly.

The patio, worked end to end

Volume (cu ft) = L × W × thickness in FEET
Cubic yards = cu ft ÷ 27

A 20 × 20 ft patio at 4 inches thick. Thickness in feet: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.333 ft. Volume: 20 × 20 × 0.333 = 133.3 cu ft. In yards: 133.3 ÷ 27 = 4.94 cubic yards. Add the 10% real-world allowance (uneven subgrade, form flex, spillage): 4.94 × 1.1 = 5.43 → order 5.5 yards of readymix.

That single number is why the unit trap matters so much: a readymix truck minimum plus a short-load fee on 59 yards is a four-figure mistake made in one form field.

Bagged concrete: the same patio by hand

An 80-lb bag yields about 0.60 cu ft. The unadjusted 133.3 cu ft ÷ 0.60 = 222 bags (223 with the waste factor, which is why the readymix truck starts looking cheap around one yard). Quick reference:

One inch of slab is 20 × 20 × (1/12) ÷ 27 = 1.23 cubic yards — about 55 bags' worth. Underspecifying thickness to "save" a yard is the most expensive saving in home construction, because thin slabs crack in exactly the places loads repeat.

Three more quiet killers

  • No waste allowance: the subgrade is never perfectly flat. 5% on a well-prepared base, 10% on anything real. A pour that comes up one wheelbarrow short is a cold joint — a permanent weak line.
  • Water for workability: a 0.5 water-cement ratio means ~25 L per 50-kg bag. Extra water makes the mix pour easy and cured concrete weak. Compact instead of souping.
  • Ignoring the short-load fee: trucks batch efficiently above ~3 yards; smaller orders often carry a fee that makes two small pours cost more than one combined one. Consolidate.

FAQ

How much concrete do I need for a 20×20 patio?

At 4 inches: 133.3 cu ft ÷ 27 = 4.94 cubic yards, plus 10% waste → order about 5.5 yards, or 222 eighty-pound bags.

Why divide by 27?

A cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet. Readymix is sold by the cubic yard, site math usually starts in feet — the ÷27 is the bridge between the two.

Is 4 inches thick enough?

For patios and walkways carrying people and garden furniture, yes. Cars want 4–5 inches with proper base prep; anything with trucks or RVs wants 5–6 inches plus rebar.

Should I order exactly what the math says?

No — order the math plus 5–10%. Running short mid-pour means a cold joint or an emergency second truck at short-load rates; the small surplus is the cheapest insurance on the job.
