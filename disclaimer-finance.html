How Our Financial Calculations Work (Methodology)

Every calculator shows its formula, a worked example with default inputs, and the assumptions behind the math — visible on the page, not hidden behind scripts. Results are computed in your browser from the values you enter, using standard published formulas (for example, standard amortization for loans and compound-interest math for savings). Where a real-world outcome depends on factors no calculator can know — your lender's exact fee schedule, your tax situation, market movements — the calculator's limitations section says so explicitly. Each calculator is covered by an automated formula QA contract with independently verified expected results before it is promoted into the sitemap.

What the Calculators Cannot See

A loan result assumes the rate stays fixed for the whole term and that every payment arrives on time. It cannot see an arrangement fee added at signing, a variable rate that resets after two years, an offset account, or a penalty for early repayment. An investment projection assumes a constant return, while real markets deliver the same average through very different sequences of good and bad years. A tax estimate assumes the rules for one jurisdiction and one year, and ignores credits, allowances and filing status changes that move the final bill.

Which Rate or Price to Enter

Use the annual rate your lender actually quoted, as a percentage, not the APR if the calculator does not ask for fees. For savings, use the rate net of any account fee. For currency, remember that a card or bank applies a spread on top of the mid-market rate shown here, typically half a percent to three percent. Entering a headline rate that ignores those costs produces an answer that is precise but optimistic.
