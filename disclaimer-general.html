How Our Estimates Are Produced

Every calculator implements a named, publicly documented formula rather than a proprietary black box: the standard loan amortisation equation, the Mifflin-St Jeor energy equation, the shoelace area formula, and so on. Each page states the formula it uses, shows a worked example with the expected output, and lists the assumptions built into the model. Arithmetic runs locally in your browser with full precision, and rounding happens only at the display step, so the number you see is reproducible by hand from the inputs shown.

Using Results Responsibly

A calculator is a checking tool, not a decision-maker. The useful pattern is to run the number twice: once with your best estimate and once with a deliberately pessimistic value. If the decision still holds at the worse number, the estimate was never load-bearing; if it flips, the input you changed is the one worth confirming with a professional or a primary document. That habit costs a minute and catches most of the errors a disclaimer can only warn about.
