AC Sizing: BTU Math and Why Oversized Units Underperform

Home & Garden · 6 min read · Last updated September 2026

Quick answer: Start from roughly 20 BTU per sq ft (hot climates run up to ~80 for uninsulated top floors): a 150 sq ft room in a hot climate needs about 12,000 BTU — a 1-ton unit. Bigger is not colder: oversized ACs short-cycle, cool unevenly and leave air clammy.

The sizing formula

Base BTU = area (sq ft) × 20 · Hot climate / poor insulation: up to × 80
Tons = BTU ÷ 12,000

Worked example: 150 sq ft bedroom, hot climate, decent insulation: 150 × 80 = 12,000 BTU → 1.0 ton. The same room in a temperate climate with good insulation might need only 150 × 20 = 3,000 BTU — climate and insulation swing the answer 4×, which is why "one rule" sizing misleads.

High ceiling / open planSize by volume, not floor area

Why oversizing fails

An oversized unit drops temperature fast, satisfies the thermostat in minutes, and shuts off — before it has dehumidified the air. Result: cold-but-clammy rooms, more on/off cycling (wear + higher peaks), and worse comfort than the "smaller" correct unit. Frequently asked questions

How many BTU do I need per square foot?

Start at 20 BTU/sq ft for temperate, well-insulated rooms; hot climates with average insulation run 60–80 BTU/sq ft. Adjust ±10% for sun exposure and add for kitchens and extra occupants.

What size AC for a 150 sq ft room?

About 12,000 BTU (1 ton) in a hot climate: 150 × 80 = 12,000. Cooler climates with good insulation may need far less — run the full formula with your conditions.

Is a bigger AC better?

No. Oversized units short-cycle: they cool quickly, shut off before dehumidifying, and deliver cold-but-clammy air with more wear. Correct sizing beats bigger.

How do I convert BTU to tons?

Divide BTU by 12,000. 12,000 BTU = 1 ton, 18,000 = 1.5 tons, 24,000 = 2 tons.
