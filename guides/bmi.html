How to Calculate BMI — Formula, Ranges & What the Number Means

Health · 5 min read · Last updated September 2026

What is BMI?

BMI (Body Mass Index) is a screening number that relates your weight to your height. It was designed as a quick population-level tool to flag underweight, healthy, overweight and obese weight categories — not as a measure of body fat or health on its own.

The BMI formula

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Worked examples

Metric — 70 kg at 1.75 m

BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9 → healthy weight range

Imperial — 154 lb at 5 ft 9 in (69 in)

BMI = 703 × 154 ÷ (69 × 69) = 703 × 154 ÷ 4761 = 108,262 ÷ 4761 ≈ 22.7 → healthy weight range

BMI ranges (WHO classification)

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Normal / healthy weight
25 – 29.9Overweight
30 and aboveObese

Classification per the World Health Organization, as used by most health authorities. Children use age- and sex-specific percentile charts instead of these adult cut-offs.

The honest limitations of BMI

  • Muscle vs fat: BMI cannot tell them apart, so muscular athletes often score "overweight" despite low body fat.
  • Age and sex: the same BMI can mean different things for different bodies.
  • Distribution matters: where fat sits (e.g. waist circumference) is often more predictive of risk than BMI alone.
  • It is a screen, not a diagnosis: BMI is a starting point — talk to a healthcare professional for a real assessment.
Important: this guide is educational and not medical advice. See our health disclaimer.

Working the formula backwards: your healthy weight range

The most useful way to use BMI is in reverse — instead of scoring your current weight, compute the weight range your height implies. Rearranged: healthy weight = BMI × height², using the range boundaries. For the 1.75 m example above:

Lower bound: 18.5 × 3.0625 = 56.7 kg · Upper bound: 24.9 × 3.0625 = 76.3 kg

So 1.75 m corresponds to a healthy range of roughly 57–76 kg — a 19 kg spread, which is why a single "target weight" is less meaningful than the range.

The same rearrangement answers "what would I weigh at BMI 22?": 22 × 3.0625 ≈ 67.4 kg. This is exactly how the ideal weight calculator works, except it draws from several published formulas that often disagree with each other — the disagreement itself is informative.

Two refinements worth knowing

  • BMI Prime — your BMI divided by 25 (the upper healthy limit). The 70 kg example scores 22.9 ÷ 25 = 0.92; anything above 1.00 is by definition outside the WHO healthy band. It's the same information in a friendlier scale: how far above or below the ceiling you sit.
  • Adjusted cut-offs for Asian populations. Several health authorities apply lower thresholds for some Asian populations — commonly overweight from 23 and obesity from 27.5 — following WHO expert consultations that found elevated health risk at lower BMIs in those groups. Check which standard your own health provider uses; the number 24.9 is not a universal constant.

One more formula exists for extremes of height: the Ponderal index (mass ÷ height³) scales differently and is sometimes preferred for neonates and very tall or short adults — the 70 kg / 1.75 m example scores 13.06. For most adults BMI remains the standard first screen; the alternatives exist because height² is an approximation, not a law.

Frequently asked questions

What is the BMI formula?

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)².

What are the BMI ranges?

Below 18.5 is underweight, 18.5–24.9 is normal/healthy weight, 25–29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese, according to the WHO classification used by most health authorities.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

No. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, so muscular athletes often score as "overweight". It is a population-level screening tool, not a body-composition measurement.

What is a healthy BMI?

The commonly used healthy range is 18.5 to 24.9. Ranges can differ slightly for children (percentile-based) and some regional guidelines.
