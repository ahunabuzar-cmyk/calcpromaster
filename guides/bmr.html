BMR: The Formula Behind Your Resting Calorie Burn

Health · 6 min read · Last updated September 2026

Quick answer: BMR is the energy your body burns at complete rest. The standard estimate is Mifflin-St Jeor: for an 80 kg, 180 cm, 30-year-old man it gives 1,780 kcal/day — before any activity. Multiply by an activity factor (1.2–1.9) to get total daily needs.

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula

Men: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

Worked example — 80 kg, 180 cm, 30-year-old male: 10 × 80 = 800; 6.25 × 180 = 1,125; 5 × 30 = 150. So 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5 = 1,780 kcal/day.

From BMR to daily needs (TDEE)

Activity levelMultiplierSame man's TDEE
Sedentary (desk job)× 1.22,136 kcal
Lightly active× 1.3752,448 kcal
Moderately active× 1.552,759 kcal
Very active× 1.7253,071 kcal

The multiplier you pick moves the answer more than any formula choice — a sedentary vs very-active pick spans 900+ kcal. Calibrate honestly: most desk workers are 1.2–1.375 even with gym visits.

Why BMR formulas disagree

The older Harris-Benedict equation gives 1,854 kcal for the same man — 74 kcal higher than Mifflin. Neither is "wrong": both are population regressions with real scatter (roughly ±200 kcal for any individual). Mifflin is preferred today because it tracks measured values somewhat better in modern populations.

Limitations: formulas estimate population averages; measured calorimetry can differ substantially, and illness, medication and muscle mass all shift real BMR. Use the estimate as a starting point and adjust against 2–3 weeks of real-world weight data.

Frequently asked questions

How is BMR calculated?

The standard Mifflin-St Jeor formula: 10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age, plus 5 for men or minus 161 for women. For an 80 kg, 180 cm, 30-year-old man that is 1,780 kcal/day.

What is a good BMR for my age?

BMR declines roughly 5 kcal per year of age in the formula (the −5 × age term). Typical adult values fall between about 1,200 and 1,900 kcal/day depending on size, sex and age — there is no single "good" number.

Should I eat my BMR to lose weight?

No. BMR is your burn at complete rest; total daily needs (BMR × activity factor) are the relevant baseline. Eating at or below BMR while active creates an unnecessarily aggressive deficit for most people.

Why do BMR and TDEE calculators give different numbers?

Different equations (Mifflin vs Harris-Benedict) and different activity multipliers. The multiplier choice usually moves the total more than the equation does — pick your activity level honestly.

What do I do with my BMR once I have it?

Multiply it by an activity factor to get your daily burn, then set your intake against that number. The ideal weight guide covers what a sensible target weight looks like, and the BMI guide explains the screening number most people pair with BMR.
