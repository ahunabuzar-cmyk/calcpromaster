Career & Freelance Guides

Part of the CalcProMaster guides library · Salary, rates & negotiating power

Whether you are employed or freelancing, the same questions repeat: what is my time worth, what does a raise actually compound to, and what rate keeps a freelance business alive after tax and downtime? These guides do the arithmetic honestly.

Methodology: Published pay, points and screening conventions (immigration points tests, ATS heuristics, negotiation bands), labelled as guidance.

Career maths turns a headline salary or rate into hourly, monthly and after-cost numbers, which is usually the point at which two apparently equal offers stop looking equal.

How the math works

Two conversions carry most career math. Hourly floor from a salary: hourly = annual ÷ 52 ÷ contracted hours — $52,000 at 40 h/week is $25/h. Freelance rate from a salary: divide by billable hours (roughly 25/week after admin, marketing and downtime), not 40 — the same $52,000 needs about $42/h to net the same money.

Raises compound because they reset every future percentage: a 5% raise on $50,000 is $2,500 this year, but that higher base compounds through every later 3–5% step — tens of thousands in cumulative difference over a decade. When negotiating, the base you lock in this year matters more than any one-off bonus, which disappears from next year’s calculation entirely.

How do I convert annual salary to hourly?

Annual ÷ paid hours. The standard baseline is ÷2080 (40 h × 52 weeks); use your real paid weeks and weekly hours for accuracy. The salary converter handles all period pairs both ways.

What should a freelance hourly rate include?

Your target net, plus taxes, plus unpaid time (admin, sales, holidays) spread over billable hours — then a margin. Charging your old salary ÷2080 quietly prices away every non-billable hour.

What this category covers

Salary versus hourly comparison, take-home pay, freelance rates, notice periods, leave and the cost of a commute. These tools answer questions that are usually decided on a feeling, which is why the numbers are worth having.

Compare like with like: gross with gross, take-home with take-home, and hours actually worked with hours actually worked. Most career calculations go wrong at that first step.

Total compensation, not base salary

A pension contribution, health cover, a training budget, paid leave and a flexible arrangement all have a cash value. Two offers that differ by 10% in base can reverse once those are priced.

Price them at what replacing them would cost you, not at what they cost the employer. That is the number that matters if you are choosing between the two.

The hours behind the number

A salaried role with 50-hour weeks pays less per hour than the same salary with 40-hour weeks. Unpaid overtime and a long commute are the two largest hidden deductions from an effective hourly rate.

Calculate the effective rate before comparing roles, and calculate it on a normal week rather than on a busy one. The normal week is what you will actually live.

Where these answers go wrong

  • Comparing an annual salary with a day rate without converting both to the same working pattern.
  • Setting a freelance rate from a target income while forgetting holidays, admin and non-billable time.
  • Comparing offers on base pay alone when allowances, pension matching and leave differ substantially.
  • Ignoring contribution thresholds when moving between employment and self-employment.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this page for "paycheck deduction calculator"?

Yes — this guide answers the "paycheck deduction calculator" question: the method is explained in the sections below and the linked calculator returns the number instantly.

What multiplier turns a day rate into an annual salary?

Use the days you can actually bill, not the days in the year. Five-day weeks minus holidays, sickness and non-billable work lands most freelancers somewhere around 200-230 billable days, so a daily rate multiplied by the full 260 overstates the annual equivalent considerably.

Why is my effective hourly rate lower than I expected?

Because paid hours are a fraction of worked hours. Quoting, invoicing, learning, travel and waiting for payment all consume time that no client pays for. Estimate the billable share honestly — if it is half, your effective hourly rate is half the headline figure, and your rate needs to reflect that.

How should I compare two job offers?

Convert both to a total annual value: base, guaranteed bonus, pension or provident matching, allowances, leave and any benefit with a cash equivalent. Then weigh the parts that do not have a number — commute, flexibility, growth. Compare totals first, then adjust for what you actually care about.
