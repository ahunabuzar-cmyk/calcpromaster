How to Calculate Concrete for Slabs, Footings and Pads

Last updated: September 2026 · Independently QA-tested formulas

Quick answer: Volume = length × width × thickness (all in feet) ÷ 27 = cubic yards. Then add 5–10% for waste. A 20×20 ft slab, 4 inches thick, needs about 4.9 cubic yards — roughly 222 bags of 80 lb mix if you're not ordering a truck.

Concrete is the classic order-too-little-and-the-pour-fails, order-too-much-and-it-hardens-in-the-truck material. The math is simple; the expensive mistakes are unit mix-ups and forgetting waste. The concrete calculator handles both — here's the hand method so you can sanity-check any quote.

The concrete volume formula

cubic feet = L (ft) × W (ft) × T (in) ÷ 12
cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27
  • L, W — slab length and width in feet.
  • T — thickness in inches (the ÷12 converts to feet).
  • ÷27 — one cubic yard = 3×3×3 = 27 cubic feet.

For footings, same formula with the trench's width and depth. For a round pad, use π × radius² × thickness.

Worked example: 20×20 patio, 4 inches thick

Step by step:

  1. Cubic feet: 20 × 20 × (4 ÷ 12) = 133.3 cu ft
  2. Cubic yards: 133.3 ÷ 27 = 4.94 cu yd
  3. Waste allowance at 10%: 4.94 × 1.10 = 5.4 cu yd to order

Mixing bags instead? One 80 lb bag yields ≈ 0.022 cu yd → 4.94 ÷ 0.022 ≈ 225 bags (round up to a full pallet — 5 bags of buffer included in the 10% rule).

Waste, subgrade and real-world buffers

Excavated soil is never perfectly flat, forms flex, and some mix always stays in the wheelbarrow. 5% covers a well-prepared base on a simple rectangle; 10% covers uneven ground, odd shapes and hand mixing. Over-ordering readymix by more than ~1 yard often triggers a short-load fee anyway, so consolidate pours where you can.

Mix ratios and the water trap

Nominal site mixes are specified by volume of cement : sand : gravel. Common grades: M10 ≈ 1:3:6, M15 ≈ 1:2:4, M20 ≈ 1:1.5:3, M25 ≈ 1:1:2 — higher cement share means higher strength. US bagged mixes are labeled in psi instead (e.g. 4,000 psi), so match the label to your project requirement rather than converting ratios on site.

Water is where DIY pours go wrong. A 0.5 water–cement ratio means about 25 litres per 50-kg bag; adding extra water makes the mix easier to pour but weakens the hardened paste. Use just enough for workability and compact instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a cubic yard of concrete cover?

At 4 inches thick, one cubic yard covers 81 sq ft (27 × 12 ÷ 4). At 3 inches, 108 sq ft; at 6 inches, 54 sq ft.

How do I calculate concrete for footings?

Trench length × width × depth, all in feet ÷ 27. A 40 ft × 1 ft × 1 ft trench = 1.48 cu yd; order 1.6 with waste.

Is bagged concrete as strong as readymix?

Quality mixes (e.g. 4000 psi blends) reach comparable strength when cured properly. The practical limits are batch consistency and fatigue — a 5-yard pour by hand is ~225 bags.

Does rebar change the volume?

Negligibly. Steel displaces some concrete, but the standard practice is to ignore it and let the waste allowance absorb the difference.
