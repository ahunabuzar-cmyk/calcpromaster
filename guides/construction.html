Construction Guides

Part of the CalcProMaster guides library · Updated September 2026

Material estimates decide whether a pour or a wall comes in on budget — or needs an emergency re-order. These guides show the volume math and the wastage buffers professionals actually use.

How the math works

Construction estimating is volume first, wastage second. Volume: length × width × thickness, kept in one unit system — a 6 m × 4 m slab at 0.1 m is 2.4 m³. Converting to bags uses the mix's cement content (a 1:2:4 mix needs roughly 6.5 bags per m³, so about 16 bags here) — the concrete guide shows the full walk-through with the common mix ratios.

Wastage is where estimates fail: 10% on concrete for spillage and over-excavation, 10% on tiles for cuts and breakage, 10% on wallpaper for pattern matching. Ordering exactly the computed volume is the classic first-project mistake — the last third of the pour arrives from a different batch, or the cut tiles for the room's awkward corner were never bought. Every guide in this section builds the buffer into the arithmetic instead of leaving it to memory.

When a job involves both materials and labour, keep the two estimates separate. Materials scale with the plan (and its wastage); labour scales with site reality — access, weather, cure times — and is quoted by experience, not by formula. The guides here deliberately cover only the material half: it's the half arithmetic can actually pin down, and the half where a 10-minute calculation reliably saves a same-day emergency purchase at whatever the local supplier charges for urgency.
