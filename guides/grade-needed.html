What Do I Need on the Final? The Weighted-Grade Formula

Education · 5 min read · Last updated September 2026

Quick answer: Required final score = (target grade − current% × final's weight as decimal) ÷ weight. Current 78% with the final worth 20% and a target of 80: (80 − 62.4) ÷ 0.2 = 88% needed on the final. The formula also honestly tells you when a target is out of reach.

The formula

Final needed % = (Target − Current × (1 − w)) ÷ w

Where w is the final's weight as a decimal. Worked example: current 78%, final worth 20% (w = 0.2), target 80%. (80 − 78 × 0.8) ÷ 0.2 = (80 − 62.4) ÷ 0.2 = 88%. Heavier finals move the requirement less than intuition suggests — a 40%-weight final for the same 80% target needs only (80 − 46.8) ÷ 0.4 = 83%.

Reading impossible answers

If the formula returns more than 100, the target is mathematically unreachable — better to know at study-plan time than at results time. Example: 60% current, 30%-weight final, 85% target: (85 − 42) ÷ 0.3 = 143% → impossible; the honest ceiling is 60 × 0.7 + 100 × 0.3 = 72%. Recalculate targets with the Grade Calculator after each marked assessment — the requirement changes every time current% moves.

Common mistakes

  • Using current % without subtracting the final's weight from its coverage (the × (1 − w) term).
  • Treating all assessments as equally weighted when the syllabus says otherwise.
  • Forgetting bonus marks that shift the effective current %.

Work backwards from the weighted total

Most courses compute a final grade as a weighted sum, so the question is not what score the last exam needs, but what weighted contribution is still missing. Convert the target grade into points, subtract what is already locked in, and divide by the weight of what remains.

If the remaining weight is small, the answer can exceed 100 and the target is arithmetically impossible. That is useful information before the exam rather than after it, because it shifts the plan to the courses where the gap can still be closed.

What counts as already locked in

Only completed work with a released grade is locked. An assignment submitted but unmarked, a participation mark awarded at the end, or a dropped lowest quiz all change the denominator, and every one of them is a common source of a wrong answer.

Ask for the syllabus weighting rather than assuming equal weights. A course where the final is 40% behaves completely differently from one where it is 15%, and students routinely plan for the wrong one.

Rounding, curving and grade boundaries

Institutions round differently: some round the final percentage, some round each component, some apply a curve after rounding. A student sitting at 89.4 can pass or fail a 90 boundary depending on which rule the registrar uses.

Treat the calculator's output as a target with a margin, not a precise threshold. Aim a point above the boundary you need, then confirm the rounding rule with the instructor.

What-if planning

Run the calculation at three outcomes for the remaining work — a realistic score, a good day and a bad day. The spread tells you whether the course is already decided or genuinely still open, which is more useful than a single number.

If even the bad-day case clears the target, spend the revision time elsewhere. If the good-day case barely clears it, that course is where the next week goes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate what I need on the final exam?

(Target − Current × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight, with the final’s weight as a decimal. 78% current, 20%-weight final, 80% target → 88% needed.

What if the grade I need is over 100%?

The target is mathematically impossible. Your realistic ceiling is current × (1 − w) + 100 × w — recompute an achievable target and put the study hours where they move other courses.

Does a heavier final make the needed score higher or lower?

Usually lower, surprisingly: a bigger weight means your current grade influences less of the outcome, so the final needs less over-performance to hit the same target.

Should I calculate this before or after each exam?

Both. Before the final it sets the study target; after every marked assessment the requirement changes, so recompute rather than relying on an old number.
