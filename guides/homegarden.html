Home & Garden Guides

Part of the CalcProMaster guides library · Rooms, materials & sizing

Home projects fail on quantities: one roll of wallpaper short, an AC that never quite cools, paint bought twice. These guides show the measurement-first approach — area, factor, waste allowance — behind every room calculation.

Methodology: Standard home and garden estimating formulas (payback, area, yield, maintenance allowances) with the assumptions stated.

Home and garden estimating is a coverage-and-waste problem: the area is easy, and the honest part of the answer is the allowance you add for offcuts, second coats and settling.

How the math works

Room calculations all start the same way: get the area right, then apply the per-unit coverage factor with a waste allowance. Paint example: a 4 m × 5 m room with 2.5 m ceilings has 45 m² of wall (perimeter 18 m × 2.5 m); at 10 m² per litre and two coats, that’s 9 litres — round up to two 5 L cans, because running out a third of the way into the second coat is a real trip.

The waste allowance is not padding: wallpaper drops get trimmed (10% typical), tiles break (buy 10% extra), lawns are never perfect rectangles. Buying exactly the computed amount is how projects end with a second shopping trip for one item — and a dye lot that doesn’t quite match.

How many rolls of wallpaper do I need?

Wall area ÷ usable area per roll, then add 10–15% for pattern matching and offcuts. Rolls rarely yield their full square metres once you align repeats — the wallpaper rolls calculator applies the allowance for you.

How is AC size (BTU) worked out?

Floor area × a climate-and-insulation factor, roughly 20 BTU per square foot as a baseline, adjusted up for sunny rooms, kitchens and high ceilings. Oversizing cools fast but leaves air clammy — size honestly.

What this category covers

Paint, flooring, tiles, grout, lawn seed, soil and fertiliser quantities. These are area-and-volume calculations, where the common failure is measuring one surface and multiplying by a rule of thumb instead of measuring each surface.

They are also the tools most likely to be used while standing in a shop, so the output needs a buying recommendation in whole units rather than a decimal quantity.

Waste allowance is not padding

Cuts, breakages, pattern matching and offcuts mean flooring and tiles need 5-10% extra, and diagonal or herringbone layouts need 10-15%. Buying exactly the measured area guarantees a second order and often a different batch.

Paint is different: a spare litre is useful for touch-ups, but five spare litres are wasted money. Buy whole cans against the computed coverage, not against the wall area.

Coverage figures are best-case

A coverage rate printed on a can or bag assumes an ideal substrate: smooth, sealed, dry and applied at the stated thickness. Rough concrete, fresh plaster and porous brick all absorb more.

For a first coat on a new surface, plan on 20-30% more material than the rated coverage. For a second coat on the same surface, the rated figure is usually closer to reality.

Where these answers go wrong

  • Using floor area for paint when walls have their own area — perimeter × height, minus windows and doors.
  • Forgetting that most finishes need two coats, and that a primer can change how far the topcoat goes.
  • Buying mulch or topsoil by area while the depth is the factor that turns area into volume.
  • Sizing an air conditioner on floor area alone when ceiling height, insulation, glazing and sun exposure drive the load.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra should I buy for waste?

For paint, plan on the number of coats plus a small margin for touch-ups, and round up to the tin sizes available. For flooring and tiling add roughly a tenth, more for a diagonal or herringbone layout and for a first attempt. Offcuts are cheaper than a second order that does not match the batch.

How do I turn area into soil volume?

Multiply area by depth in the same units. A 10 m² bed at 5 cm deep needs 0.5 m³, and at 20 cm it needs 2 m³. Bags are usually sold by volume, so convert once and you can compare bags against a bulk delivery on price without guessing.

Why is air-conditioner sizing not just square metres?

Because two rooms of the same size can need very different cooling. Ceiling height changes the volume of air, insulation and glazing change how much heat enters, and orientation changes the solar load. Square metres is a starting sanity check, not the sizing calculation.
