Regional Finance (India & South Asia) Guides

Part of the CalcProMaster guides library · FD, RD, PPF, SIP & local tax

South-Asian savers juggle instruments the rest of the world doesn’t use: quarterly-compounded FDs, PPF’s annual ceiling, SIP instalments, TDS. These guides do the regional math with the regional rules.

Methodology: Region-specific rules and rates for India, Pakistan and the Gulf, sourced from published rates and clearly labelled as estimates that change.

Regional calculators pair a local instrument with local rules, and those rules move: rates, slabs and limits are revised, so every result is only current for the period it models.

How the math works

Regional instruments have their own compounding conventions, and using the wrong one misprices them. An Indian FD at 7% “p.a.” usually compounds quarterly: effective yield = (1 + 0.07/4)⁴ − 1 ≈ 7.19%. SIP returns compound monthly and depend on entry timing, which is why the same SIP shows a different XIRR for every investor.

Tax treatment differs just as sharply: PPF interest is tax-free and the deposit deductible, while FD interest is taxable at slab rate. A 7% FD in the 30% bracket nets about 4.9% after tax — less than PPF’s tax-free 7.1%. The regional calculators encode these rules; the guides show the arithmetic so you can verify each step.

How is FD maturity calculated?

A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t) with quarterly compounding (n = 4) for most Indian bank FDs. A ₹100,000 FD at 7% for 5 years compounds to roughly ₹141,478 — the FD calculator applies your bank’s exact rate and tenure.

What is the PPF interest rule?

Interest accrues monthly on the lowest balance between the 5th and last day of each month, credits annually, and compounds yearly. Depositing before the 5th preserves the month’s interest — the PPF calculator follows this convention.

Local units are not conversions of metric units

A marla, a kanal, a bigha, a maund and a tola are traditional units whose exact size varies by region and sometimes by district. Unlike a mile, they have no single international definition, so a figure copied from another city can be wrong by a double-digit percentage.

Check the local standard before converting. Where a calculator offers a regional unit, it states the definition it used; where it does not, treat the answer as approximate.

Large-number names differ by system

Lakh and crore are 100,000 and 10,000,000 in the South Asian system, while million and billion follow the short scale used in most English-speaking countries. A figure of 1.2 billion is 120 crore, not 12 crore.

When sending a number across systems, write the digits as well as the word. That single habit prevents most of the expensive misunderstandings in cross-border contracts.

Local tax and payroll rules change the answer

Take-home pay, sales tax and property charges are set locally and change with each budget. A calculator that does not name the tax year is giving you a historical answer.

Use the guide's stated assumptions as a checklist, then confirm the current rates with the authority. The structure of the calculation travels; the rates do not.

Where these answers go wrong

  • Applying the current year’s slabs or rates to a past year, or a past year’s to the current one.
  • Pairing an annual rate with the wrong compounding frequency — quarterly and monthly recurring deposits differ.
  • Reading an advertised return as guaranteed when it is a ceiling, an average or a historical figure.
  • Comparing instruments without accounting for how each is taxed and how long the money is locked in.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a fixed deposit and a recurring deposit?

A fixed deposit puts one lump sum in for a term, so interest compounds on the whole amount from day one. A recurring deposit adds a fixed amount every month, so each instalment earns for a different length of time. Same rate, different total interest — the recurring deposit earns roughly half on the same nominal principal.

Why do provident and small-savings rates change?

Governments reset them periodically against a reference — usually government bond yields or a formula tied to them. That is why a plan built on today’s rate is a projection, not a promise. Model a lower rate too, and check whether the plan still holds if the rate resets downwards.

How much does compounding frequency matter?

Less than people expect at the same stated rate. Going from annual to quarterly compounding raises the effective annual yield by a small fraction of a percent, while going from 6% to 7% changes the outcome far more. Frequency matters most when the term is long and the amount large, and it rarely changes which instrument wins.
