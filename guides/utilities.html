Utility tools deserve one explanation each: what a UUID actually guarantees, why password strength is entropy rather than length theatre, and when a hash is a fingerprint versus a password. These guides cover the concepts the tools rely on.

Methodology: Standard utility formulas such as energy use, cost per unit and efficiency, using published tariffs as inputs only.

Utility calculators answer small questions where the unit convention is the whole answer: bits versus bytes, decimal versus binary prefixes, and what a "strong" password is actually measuring.

How the math works

Security utilities are honest when the limits are stated. A random UUIDv4 carries 122 random bits — collision odds across a billion UUIDs are roughly one in a quintillion, so you will never see one. Password entropy is bits = log2(pool size) × length: 16 lowercase letters give about 75 bits; 8 characters from the full keyboard give about 52. Length wins because the formula multiplies, not adds.

Hashes are for verifying data, not for storing passwords: a hash is fast by design, which is exactly what an attacker wants. That is why the password tools generate client-side with cryptographically secure randomness and never transmit anything — the tool’s honesty is in what it refuses to do, not just what it computes.

What makes a generated password strong?

Entropy in bits: log₂(pool size) × length. A 16-character password from a 62-character pool carries about 95 bits — far beyond offline-cracking reach. The passwords guide shows the full table.

Are UUIDs safe as unguessable IDs?

Version-4 UUIDs carry 122 random bits, so guessing one is effectively impossible. But they are not secrets — anything that must resist guessing AND stay confidential still needs authentication on top.

What this category covers

Electricity, gas and water bills, plus appliance running costs and tariff comparisons. The arithmetic is simple, but the tariff structure rarely is: slabs, fixed charges, duties and time-of-use rates all change what a unit actually costs.

Read one real bill before using any calculator in this category. The fixed portion and the tax lines explain most of the gap between an estimate and what arrives in the post.

Slabs make the last unit the expensive one

In a slab tariff, each band has its own rate, so the bill is the sum of bands rather than units times one price. The rate on your final unit can be several times the rate on your first.

That is why adding a large appliance often costs more than a per-unit estimate suggests, and why reducing usage in a high band saves more per unit than reducing it in a low one.

Appliance estimates and duty cycles

Watts times hours is the formula, but nameplate watts are a maximum. A fridge cycles, an inverter air conditioner modulates, and a laptop idles well below its charger rating.

For anything that runs for hours, measure it for a week with a plug-in meter. For a phone charger or a router, the label is close enough and the total is too small to justify the effort.

Where these answers go wrong

  • Reading a connection speed in megabits as if it were megabytes — divide by eight before estimating how long a download takes.
  • Comparing a drive’s advertised capacity (decimal) with what the operating system reports (binary), which differ by roughly 9%.
  • Judging a password by character classes alone rather than by the search space its length creates.
  • Converting colour codes without checking the working colour space, so the hex value looks right and the rendered colour does not.

Frequently asked questions

What download speed do I need for a 4 GB file in 10 minutes?

Work backwards in consistent units. 4 GB is about 32,000 megabits, and 10 minutes is 600 seconds, so you need roughly 53 Mbps of sustained throughput. Protocol overhead and server limits mean the connection should be comfortably faster than the number you calculate.

Why does a 1 TB drive show less than 1 TB?

Drive makers use decimal prefixes, where 1 TB means 1,000 GB, while many operating systems report binary units, where 1 TiB means 1,024 GiB. Both numbers describe the same space. The visible shortfall is the gap between the two conventions, not missing storage.

Does a password strength meter measure entropy?

Most measure a checklist plus an estimate of the search space implied by length and character set, which is not the same as true entropy. Length raises the search space fastest, which is why a long passphrase usually beats a short scramble of symbols — and why any password reused elsewhere should be considered weak.
